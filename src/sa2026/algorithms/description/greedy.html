Greedy Best-First Search

Intelligent Search Algorithms · Latakia University · Lesson 2 of 3: Heuristics → Greedy → A*

The idea

You walk to a tower that you can see. At every corner you take the street that goes most toward the tower. Easy and fast! But sometimes that street ends at a river…

Greedy in one sentence: always expand the node that looks closest to the goal: the node with the smallest guess h(n).
f(n) = h(n)

Greedy uses only the guess h. It does not use g, the cost it already paid. A long, expensive road does not worry it.

Not sure about h, h*, admissible? Read lesson 1: heuristics.html.

On our map

At S, Greedy sees two children: A with h = 8 and B with h = 4. B looks closer, so Greedy goes to B. It does not see that the road S – B is long, and that the way after B is long too.

Greedy is greedy: it takes what looks best now, by the guess only.

    Is Greedy good?
    
        
    m = the maximum depth of the search tree. b = the branching factor.
    
        
    
          
    QuestionAnswerWhy
    
          
    Complete?
    Does it find a solution if one exists?    		
            Tree search: No
    Graph search: Yes*    		
            Tree search can go around a loop forever (try the misleading guess in step 2).
              Graph search never expands a city twice, so in a finite map it stops.
    
          
    Optimal?
    Is the solution the cheapest?    		
            NoIt never looks at g. On our map it pays 21 km, but 18 km is possible.
    
          
    TimeO(bm)Worst case. A good guess makes it much faster.
    
          
    SpaceO(bm)It keeps all the nodes in memory.
    
        
    
        
    * if the number of states is finite.
    
        
    Good point: with a good guess, Greedy is often very fast.
          On our map it expanded only  cities (UCS: ).
    
      
    

      
    
        
    
          
    Does a better heuristic help?
    
          
    For speed: yes. For complete and optimal: almost no.

    * if the number of states is finite (and every road costs more than 0).
    
          
    Why can a consistent h still loop? Two cities X and Y next to each other, both with h = 1.
            The goal is behind a long road. Greedy goes X → Y → X → Y… because 1 is always the smallest h.
    
          
    Why is even a perfect h not optimal? City X is 1 km from the goal,
            but the road to X is 100 km long. Greedy loves X (h = 1) and pays 101 km.
            (On our city map, h = h* is lucky and finds 18 km. Try to make a map where it is not lucky!)
    
          
    Remember: Greedy is never optimal, with any heuristic.
            Admissible and consistent do not help Greedy. They are for A*.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    The loop in tree search
    
          
    Misleading guess: h(A) = 2, h(C) = 1. Greedy goes S → A → C. The children of C are A (h = 2) and F (h = 6).
            A looks better, so it goes back to A. Then to C. Then to A…
    
          
    A→C→A→C→… forever
    
          
    Graph search remembers that A and C are explored, so it skips them and goes on.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Greedy in short
    
          
    Good: fast with a good guess. Simple.
    
          
    Bad: not optimal (with any h). Tree search can loop.
    
          
    The fix: also count what we already paid (g). f = g + h. That is A*.
